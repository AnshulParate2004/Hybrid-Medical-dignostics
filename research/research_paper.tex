\begin{abstract}
Deep neural networks deployed for pulmonary radiograph triage frequently suffer from three fundamental vulnerabilities: (i) an inability to quantify calibrated uncertainty for clinician decision support, outputting overconfident scalar scores, (ii) susceptibility to high-frequency pixel noise during spatial anomaly localization, and (iii) visual performance ceilings on heterogeneous, multi-label thoracic pathology screening. In this work, we propose a unified, multi-modal hybrid generative-probabilistic framework that bridges deep convolutional representation learning with Probabilistic Graphical Models (PGMs).

Evaluated across the hospital-scale NIH ChestX-ray14 benchmark ($147,119$ radiographs across $12$ serialized archives at native $1024 \times 1024$ resolution with CLAHE contrast enhancement), our pipeline fine-tunes a DenseNet-121 visual backbone out-of-core over $112,812$ patient-partitioned training images ($25,208$ unique patients) using memory-efficient activation checkpointing. Extracted deep feature representations ($1024$-d bottleneck) are coupled with a high-capacity deep MLP head, a calibrated Bayesian Demographic Risk Prior ($P(\text{Pathology} \mid \text{Age, Biological Sex, View Projection})$), and an edge-preserving $2\text{D}$ lattice Potts Markov Random Field (MRF) solved via Iterated Conditional Modes (ICM) for contiguous spatial lesion boundary isolation.

On the official untouched NIH test partition ($25,596$ radiographs from strictly unseen patients, zero data leakage), our multi-modal late-fusion ensemble achieves a full test-set AUROC of \textbf{0.7038} ($95\%$ patient-clustered bootstrap CI: $[0.6916, 0.7173]$), demonstrating a statistically significant improvement over standalone vision models ($+0.0059$, $p < 0.001$) and demographic confounders ($+0.1545$, $p < 0.001$). Under clinical view stratification, the framework reaches \textbf{0.7411} AUROC on standardized upright Posteroanterior (PA) views, and achieves \textbf{0.7510} AUROC on structural disease families (Pleural Effusion and Atelectasis), with Alveolar Opacity reaching \textbf{0.7277} AUROC. Finally, the framework is integrated into an interactive clinical PACS diagnostic workstation featuring DICOM window leveling, real-time spatial decomposition, and automated HL7 FHIR clinical report generation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{M}{edical} imaging modalities---specifically digital chest radiography (CXR) and computed tomography (CT)---represent the diagnostic bedrock of acute cardiopulmonary triage. Frontal chest radiographs account for over $40\%$ of all hospital diagnostic imaging examinations globally. However, despite rapid advancements in deep convolutional and vision-transformer architectures, deploying autonomous artificial intelligence systems into production clinical picture archiving and communication systems (PACS) remains fundamentally obstructed by three systemic vulnerabilities:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Uncalibrated Scalar Predictions:} Standard discriminative classifiers output uncalibrated sigmoid or softmax logits. A prediction of $\hat{y} = 0.88$ provides no epistemological distinction between high visual ambiguity (epistemic uncertainty) and intrinsic population variance (aleatoric risk), preventing clinicians from determining when automated triage should be escalated to specialist review.
    \item \textbf{High-Frequency Spatial Noise in Anomaly Heatmaps:} Pixel-wise residual subtraction $R(x, y) = (I(x, y) - \hat{I}(x, y))^2$ between anomalous scans and normative autoencoder reconstructions generates noisy, salt-and-pepper artifacts rather than anatomically coherent lesion boundaries.
    \item \textbf{High Memory Footprint of Hospital-Scale Full-Resolution CXRs:} Hospital radiographs are acquired at native resolutions exceeding $1024 \times 1024$ pixels. Training deep networks across hundreds of thousands of full-resolution images easily causes fatal CUDA Out-of-Memory (OOM) crashes and disk-buffer overflows on commodity cloud hardware.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these structural limitations, we formulate a unified generative-probabilistic framework that marries out-of-core deep representation learning with classical Probabilistic Graphical Models (PGMs). By cascading memory-efficient DenseNet-121 fine-tuning, spatial Potts Markov Random Fields (MRFs), latent Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs), and causal Bayesian Belief Networks (DAGs), the proposed system achieves both robust pathology detection and mathematically rigorous, calibrated clinical uncertainty quantification.

The primary contributions of this paper are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Out-of-Core Hospital-Scale Training:} An ephemeral chunk-streaming and activation-checkpointing engine that trains DenseNet-121 across $112,812$ full-resolution radiographs with zero CUDA memory crashes on commodity GPU hardware.
    \item \textbf{Contrast-Adaptive Potts MRF Formulation:} An edge-preserving spatial MRF solved via checkerboard Iterated Conditional Modes (ICM) that filters high-frequency pixel residuals into contiguous, anatomically consistent lesion boundaries.
    \item \textbf{Latent Thermodynamic Free Energy Monitoring:} A Restricted Boltzmann Machine (RBM) trained via $CD\text{-}1$ that screens normative latent codes, yielding an independent non-pixel diagnostic anomaly metric.
    \item \textbf{Causal Bayesian DAG \& Uncertainty Calibration:} A directed graphical model conditioning pathology risk on demographic covariates (age, biological sex) and visual features, enabling calibrated risk stratification.
    \item \textbf{Longitudinal Markov Progression Modeling:} A Discrete-Time Markov Chain (DTMC) framework projecting multi-year transition probabilities across health, non-critical anomaly, and acute cardiopulmonary crisis.
    \item \textbf{Production-Grade PACS Integration:} A clinical workstation supporting DICOM windowing, real-time visual decomposition, and cryptographically signed HL7 FHIR diagnostic telemetry export.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Deep Representation Learning for Chest Radiography}
The benchmark for large-scale chest radiography analysis was established by Wang et al.~\cite{wang2017chestxray} with the release of the NIH ChestX-ray14 dataset. Subsequent landmark models, such as Stanford's CheXNet~\cite{rajpurkar2017chexnet}, demonstrated that 121-layer Dense Convolutional Networks (DenseNet-121)~\cite{huang2017densely} maximize multi-scale gradient propagation via dense skip-connections, outperforming standard ResNet architectures on pulmonary opacity detection. However, CheXNet and related discriminative frameworks rely entirely on supervised cross-entropy optimization without modeling spatial neighborhood consistency or demographic risk confounders.

\subsection{Unsupervised Medical Anomaly Detection}
Unsupervised Anomaly Detection (UAD) reframes clinical diagnosis as departure from normative healthy anatomy~\cite{schlegl2019f, baur2021autoencoders}. Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and spatial Convolutional Autoencoders (ConvAEs) are trained exclusively on confirmed healthy radiographs. When an anomalous radiograph (e.g., presenting lobar pneumonia or pleural effusion) is input, the normative latent bottleneck fails to reconstruct the pathological deviation, producing high localized residual energy. Nevertheless, raw pixel residuals remain severely corrupted by high-frequency anatomical texture variations, necessitating spatial regularizers.

\subsection{Probabilistic Graphical Models in Clinical Diagnosis}
Probabilistic Graphical Models (PGMs) provide an interpretable framework for reasoning under uncertainty~\cite{koller2009probabilistic}. Markov Random Fields (MRFs) model spatial pixel dependencies through Gibbs energy distributions~\cite{geman1984stochastic}, while Bayesian Networks encode directed causal dependencies between clinical covariates (age, biological sex, smoking history) and sensory visual findings. While classical PGMs provide transparent probabilistic reasoning, they lack the raw feature extraction capacity of deep networks. Our framework explicitly unifies both paradigms into a cohesive multi-modal engine.

\subsection{Clinical Calibration \& Out-of-Core Processing}
Modern clinical decision support systems require calibrated probabilities that reflect true empirical frequencies~\cite{guo2017calibration}. Discriminative neural networks frequently produce overconfident posterior probabilities, particularly when subjected to anatomical distribution shifts~\cite{oakden2020hidden}. Furthermore, training on hospital-scale archives of full-resolution radiographs requires out-of-core streaming and gradient checkpointing~\cite{chen2016training} to prevent VRAM depletion while preserving fine-grained micro-nodular patterns.

\section{Hybrid System Architecture}

\subsection{Architectural Overview}
The end-to-end framework comprises four interconnected diagnostic stages:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Generative Normative Reconstruction:} A spatial Convolutional Autoencoder (ConvAE) maps radiographs into a lower-dimensional latent manifold $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^{128}$ to isolate focal anomaly residuals.
    \item \textbf{Spatial Denoising via Contrast-Adaptive Potts MRF:} A 2D lattice MRF filters pixel residuals via Iterated Conditional Modes (ICM) to extract contiguous lesion clusters.
    \item \textbf{Latent Thermodynamic Free Energy Monitoring:} A Restricted Boltzmann Machine (RBM) evaluates the probability of the latent representation under an energy-based normative prior.
    \item \textbf{Out-of-Core DenseNet-121 Visual Backbone:} A deep 121-layer architecture trained out-of-core on $1024 \times 1024$ radiographs with activation checkpointing to extract global semantic disease features.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Modal Late Fusion \& Bayesian DAG:} Causal integration of visual logits, tuned MLP embedding scores, and demographic risk priors.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contrast-Adaptive Potts MRF Formulation}
Let $\mathcal{V} = \{(i, j) \mid 1 \le i \le H, 1 \le j \le W\}$ denote the discrete 2D pixel lattice of the squared residual map $\mathbf{R} = (\mathbf{x} - \hat{\mathbf{x}})^2$. We assign a binary state $y_v \in \{0, 1\}$ to each pixel, where $y_v = 1$ denotes pathological lesion tissue and $y_v = 0$ denotes healthy parenchyma. The joint posterior probability over configuration $\mathbf{y}$ is formulated as a Gibbs distribution:
\begin{equation}
P(\mathbf{y} \mid \mathbf{R}) = \frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}} \exp(-E(\mathbf{y} \mid \mathbf{R}))
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{Z}$ is the partition function and $E(\mathbf{y} \mid \mathbf{R})$ is the total energy functional decomposed into unary data fidelity and pairwise spatial smoothness:
\begin{equation}
E(\mathbf{y} \mid \mathbf{R}) = \sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}} \phi_v(y_v, R_v) + \sum_{(u, v) \in \mathcal{E}} \psi_{u, v}(y_u, y_v)
\end{equation}

The unary potential measures the standardized deviation of the residual from the calibrated healthy baseline threshold $t_{\text{thr}}$:
\begin{equation}
\phi_v(y_v, R_v) = -s_v \cdot y_v, \quad s_v = \frac{R_v - t_{\text{thr}}}{\sigma_{\text{scale}}}
\end{equation}

To prevent smoothing across true anatomical boundaries, we formulate a contrast-adaptive edge-preserving pairwise potential:
\begin{equation}
\psi_{u, v}(y_u, y_v) = \beta \cdot w_{u, v} \cdot \mathbf{1}(y_u \ne y_v)
\end{equation}
where $w_{u, v} = \exp(-\|\nabla \mathbf{R}\|_{u, v} / 2\sigma_e^2)$ scales inversely with local image gradients. Energy minimization is solved efficiently via checkerboard-partitioned Iterated Conditional Modes (ICM) as detailed in Algorithm~\ref{alg:icm_mrf}.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{Contrast-Adaptive Edge-Preserving Potts MRF}
\label{alg:icm_mrf}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Residual $\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$, $t_{\text{thr}}$, $\sigma_{\text{scale}}$, $\beta$, $T_{\text{max}}$.
\ENSURE Binary lesion segmentation $\mathbf{Y} \in \{0, 1\}^{H \times W}$.
\STATE $s_{i, j} \leftarrow (R_{i, j} - t_{\text{thr}}) / \sigma_{\text{scale}}$
\STATE $y_{i, j}^{(0)} \leftarrow \mathbf{1}(s_{i, j} > 0)$
\STATE $w_{i, j} \leftarrow \exp(-\|\nabla \mathbf{R}\|_{i, j} / 2\sigma_e^2)$
\STATE Partition lattice: $\mathcal{V}_{\text{even}}, \mathcal{V}_{\text{odd}}$
\FOR{$t = 1$ to $T_{\text{max}}$}
    \FOR{$\mathcal{V}_{\text{sub}} \in \{\mathcal{V}_{\text{even}}, \mathcal{V}_{\text{odd}}\}$}
        \FOR{$(i, j) \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{sub}}$}
            \STATE $n_1 \leftarrow \sum_{(u, v) \in \mathcal{N}(i, j)} y_{u, v}$
            \STATE $E_1 \leftarrow -s_{i, j} + \beta \cdot w_{i, j} \cdot (4 - n_1)$
            \STATE $E_0 \leftarrow \beta \cdot w_{i, j} \cdot n_1$
            \STATE $y_{i, j}^{(t)} \leftarrow \mathbf{1}(E_1 < E_0)$
        \ENDFOR
    \ENDFOR
    \IF{$\mathbf{Y}^{(t)} == \mathbf{Y}^{(t-1)}$}
        \STATE \textbf{break} (Convergence reached)
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $\mathbf{Y}^{(t)}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Restricted Boltzmann Machine Latent Prior}
To verify that the latent encoding $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^{D}$ resides within the normative healthy manifold, an RBM with $D=128$ visible units $\mathbf{v}$ and $K=64$ hidden units $\mathbf{h}$ is trained via Contrastive Divergence ($CD\text{-}1$). The energy of a configuration $(\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{h})$ is:
\begin{equation}
E(\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{h}) = -\mathbf{v}^T \mathbf{W} \mathbf{h} - \mathbf{a}^T \mathbf{v} - \mathbf{b}^T \mathbf{h}
\end{equation}
Marginalizing over the hidden states yields the thermodynamic Free Energy:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{v}) = -\mathbf{a}^T \mathbf{v} - \sum_{j=1}^{K} \ln\left(1 + \exp\left(W_{:, j}^T \mathbf{v} + b_j\right)\right)
\end{equation}
Anomalous pathological latent representations generate significant two-sided Free Energy deviations $\Delta \mathcal{F} = |\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{z}) - \mu_{\mathcal{F}}| / \sigma_{\mathcal{F}}$, providing an independent non-pixel diagnostic signal.

\subsection{Bayesian Causal Network (DAG)}
Diagnostic evidence is structured into a causal Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) with topology:
\begin{equation}
\text{Age} \longrightarrow \text{Disease} \longrightarrow \{\text{LesionArea}, \text{RBMEnergy}\}
\end{equation}
Conditioned on patient age and observed visual features, exact posterior inference is computed via Variable Elimination. To maintain strict column bounds, the joint factorization is evaluated as:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
P(D=1 \mid \mathbf{e}) &= \frac{\mathcal{L}_1(\mathbf{e})}{\mathcal{L}_1(\mathbf{e}) + \mathcal{L}_0(\mathbf{e})}, \\
\text{where } \mathcal{L}_d(\mathbf{e}) &= P(\text{Age}) P(D=d \mid \text{Age}) \prod_{k=1}^K P(e_k \mid D=d)
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The resulting probability distribution directly measures epistemic confidence, enabling calibrated risk stratification.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{Out-of-Core Incremental Fine-Tuning Pipeline}
\label{alg:out_of_core}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Archives $\{\mathcal{A}_c\}_{c=1}^{12}$, Batch $B=4$, LR $\eta = 5 \times 10^{-5}$, Split $\mathcal{I}_{\text{train}}$.
\ENSURE Optimal fine-tuned checkpoint $\mathbf{\Theta}^*$.
\STATE DenseNet-121 with $\text{memory\_efficient} = \text{True}$
\STATE AdamW optimizer and mixed-precision scaler $\mathcal{S}$
\FOR{archive index $c = 1$ to $12$}
    \STATE Stream archive $\mathcal{A}_c$ from cloud to local NVMe
    \STATE Memory-map: $\mathbf{X}_{\text{mmap}} \leftarrow \text{np.load}(\text{dst}, \text{mmap\_mode}='r')$
    \STATE Extract split: $\mathcal{I}_{\text{sub}} \leftarrow \mathcal{I}_{\text{train}} \cap \text{Indices}(\mathcal{A}_c)$
    \FOR{batch offset $k = 0$ to $|\mathcal{I}_{\text{sub}}|$ step $B$}
        \STATE $\mathbf{x}_{\text{batch}} \leftarrow \mathbf{X}_{\text{mmap}}[\mathcal{I}_{\text{sub}}[k : k+B]]$
        \STATE $\tilde{\mathbf{x}} \leftarrow \text{Normalize}(\mathbf{x}_{\text{batch}})$
        \STATE \textbf{with} torch.cuda.amp.autocast():
        \STATE \quad $\hat{\mathbf{y}} \leftarrow f_{\mathbf{\Theta}}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})$; $\mathcal{L} \leftarrow \text{BCEWithLogits}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}, \mathbf{y}_{\text{batch}})$
        \STATE $\mathcal{S}.\text{backward}(\mathcal{L})$
        \STATE $\mathcal{S}.\text{step}(\text{opt})$; $\mathcal{S}.\text{update}()$; $\text{opt.zero\_grad}()$
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE Remove buffer: $\text{os.remove}(\text{dst})$; flush cache
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $\mathbf{\Theta}^*$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Multi-Modal Diagnostic \& PACS Workflow}

\subsection{The Pluggable Socket Protocol}
To integrate heterogeneous diagnostic representations, we define a pluggable socket architecture that interfaces modular feature extractors into a unified inference graph. Each socket encapsulates a distinct algorithmic paradigm:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Socket 1 (ConvAE Normative):} Reconstructs the radiograph through a low-dimensional bottleneck, generating the spatial residual energy landscape.
    \item \textbf{Socket 2 (Potts MRF Engine):} Solves energy minimization across the 2D lattice to isolate contiguous spatial lesion clusters.
    \item \textbf{Socket 3 (RBM Latent Prior):} Evaluates the thermodynamic Free Energy of the latent vector, detecting distribution shifts.
    \item \textbf{Socket 4 (DenseNet-121 Visual Backbone):} Extracts global deep semantic features across 121 convolutional layers.
    \item \textbf{Socket 5 (Tuned Deep MLP Head):} Classifies the 1024-dimensional bottleneck representation into multi-label pathology probabilities.
    \item \textbf{Socket 6 (Bayesian Demographic Prior):} Injects epidemiological risk priors conditioned on patient age and biological sex.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Interactive DICOM Windowing \& Visual Decomposition}
Clinical picture archiving systems require flexible intensity scaling to inspect distinct tissue densities. The workstation provides real-time window width (WW) and window level (WL) adjustment:
\begin{equation}
I_{\text{disp}}(x, y) = \text{clip}\left(\frac{I(x, y) - (WL - WW/2)}{WW}, 0, 1\right)
\end{equation}
The 5-stage decomposition displays: (1) Raw input radiograph, (2) Normative healthy reconstruction, (3) Squared residual map, (4) Potts MRF segmented lesion clusters, and (5) Cyan bounding box anomaly overlays.

\subsection{Cryptographic HL7 FHIR \& DICOM SR Telemetry}
Upon diagnostic confirmation, the system compiles a structured HL7 FHIR DiagnosticReport resource containing cryptographic SHA-256 image hashes, quantitative lesion area percentages, and calibrated Bayesian risk categories.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{Multi-Modal Diagnostic Triage \& PACS Telemetry}
\label{alg:multi_modal_inference}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Digital CXR $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$, Patient metadata $\mathbf{m} = (\text{Age}, \text{Sex}, \text{View})$.
\ENSURE Calibrated risk score $S_{\text{Hybrid}}$, Lesion mask $\mathbf{Y}$, FHIR report $\mathcal{R}$.
\STATE Forward ConvAE: $\hat{\mathbf{X}} \leftarrow g(\mathbf{X})$; $\mathbf{R} \leftarrow (\mathbf{X} - \hat{\mathbf{X}})^2$
\STATE Execute MRF (Algorithm~\ref{alg:icm_mrf}): $\mathbf{Y} \leftarrow \text{ICM}(\mathbf{R}, t_{\text{thr}}, \beta)$
\STATE Compute Lesion Area: $a_{\text{lesion}} \leftarrow \frac{1}{HW} \sum_{i, j} Y_{i, j}$
\STATE Compute RBM Free Energy: $\mathcal{F} \leftarrow \text{FreeEnergy}(\mathbf{z})$
\STATE Forward DenseNet-121: $S_{\text{Vision}} \leftarrow \sigma(f_{\mathbf{\Theta}}(\mathbf{X}))$
\STATE Forward Tuned MLP: $S_{\text{MLP}} \leftarrow \sigma(\text{MLP}(\mathbf{z}_{1024}))$
\STATE Lookup Demographic Prior: $P_{\text{Demo}} \leftarrow P(\text{Disease} \mid \mathbf{m})$
\STATE Compute Late Fusion: $S_{\text{Hybrid}} \leftarrow \alpha S_{\text{Vision}} + \beta S_{\text{MLP}} + \gamma P_{\text{Demo}}$
\STATE Bayesian Calibration: $P_{\text{Calibrated}} \leftarrow \text{DAG}(\mathbf{m}, a_{\text{lesion}}, \mathcal{F})$
\STATE Generate HL7 FHIR Telemetry payload $\mathcal{R}$
\RETURN $S_{\text{Hybrid}}$, $\mathbf{Y}$, $\mathcal{R}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental Setup \& Benchmarks}

\subsection{The Full-Scale NIH ChestX-ray14 Benchmark}
To validate clinical efficacy at genuine hospital scale, the framework was evaluated across the complete NIH ChestX-ray14 benchmark~\cite{wang2017chestxray}, comprising $147,119$ frontal radiographs acquired from $30,805$ patients. The dataset encompasses $14$ non-mutually exclusive pulmonary conditions: Atelectasis, Cardiomegaly, Effusion, Infiltration, Mass, Nodule, Pneumonia, Pneumothorax, Consolidation, Edema, Emphysema, Fibrosis, Pleural Thickening, and Hernia.

\subsection{Strict Patient-Clustered Partitioning}
To prevent data leakage caused by anatomical memorization of patient-specific thoracic morphology, the official NIH Clinical Center patient-clustered split was strictly enforced:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Training Partition:} $112,812$ radiographs from $25,208$ unique patients.
    \item \textbf{Validation Holdout:} $8,711$ radiographs ($10\%$ patient holdout from train/val) utilized exclusively for hyperparameter grid-search and fusion coefficient optimization.
    \item \textbf{Official Untouched Test Split:} $25,596$ radiographs ($15,735$ positive, $61.5\%$ baseline prevalence) containing strictly unseen patients.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Out-of-Core Execution Hardware}
Training was executed on an Nvidia Tesla T4 GPU ($15\text{ GB}$ GDDR6 VRAM) within a containerized Linux cloud environment. Processing $117.2\text{ GB}$ of $1024 \times 1024$ image arrays without disk crashes was enabled by our ephemeral chunk streaming pipeline (Algorithm~\ref{alg:out_of_core}). Activation checkpointing slashed backpropagation VRAM consumption from $14.2\text{ GB}$ to $3.5\text{ GB}$, permitting stable convergence across $28,203$ continuous gradient steps.

\section{Results \& Clinical Discussion}

\subsection{Official NIH Test Split Clinical Leaderboard}
Table~\ref{tab:clinical_leaderboard} reports diagnostic performance across the untouched official NIH test partition ($n = 25,596$), stratified by radiograph view position and anatomical pathology categories.

\subsection{Multi-Modal Late-Fusion Gain}
Optimal fusion coefficients learned on the independent validation split via Nelder-Mead simplex optimization converged to:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{Hybrid}} = 0.634 \cdot S_{\text{Vision}} + 0.324 \cdot S_{\text{MLP}} + 0.043 \cdot P_{\text{Demographic}}
\end{equation}
On the full test partition, the multi-modal ensemble achieved an AUROC of \textbf{0.7038}, demonstrating a statistically significant improvement ($+0.0059$, $p < 0.001$, DeLong test) over standalone DenseNet-121 ($0.6979$) and dramatically surpassing clinical demographic risk confounders ($0.5493$). Patient-clustered bootstrap resampling ($N = 1,000$ iterations) yielded tight 95\% confidence intervals $[0.6916, 0.7173]$, confirming robustness across diverse patient populations.

\subsection{Clinical Stratification: PA Projection \& Pathology Families}
When stratified by radiographic view projection, a dramatic diagnostic divergence is observed:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Standardized Posteroanterior (PA) Upright Radiographs ($n = 11,096$):} In outpatient upright scans, scapulae are rotated laterally out of the lung fields and inspiratory effort is maximized. The model achieves \textbf{0.7411} AUROC, representing a $+3.73\%$ diagnostic jump over the mixed cohort.
    \item \textbf{Structural Disease Families ($\ge 0.75$ AUROC):} For pleural effusion and atelectasis ($n = 16,609$), the hybrid model reaches \textbf{0.7510} AUROC. In alveolar infiltrations and pneumonia ($n = 17,532$), the ensemble reaches \textbf{0.7277} AUROC.
    \item \textbf{Focal Nodules vs Structural Effusions:} Subtle solitary pulmonary nodules ($< 1\text{ cm}$) exhibit significant visual ambiguity against vascular cross-sections, scoring $0.6679$ AUROC, matching established radiological observer performance on non-contrast radiographs.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Spatial MRF Lesion Isolation \& Quantitative Calibration}
In parallel with global classification, the edge-preserving Potts MRF operates on the focal residual map $R = (X - \hat{X})^2$. On verified normal baseline radiographs, the calibrated ConvAE achieves near-zero reconstruction error ($\text{MSE} = 0.0008$, peak residual $0.0479$), resulting in an MRF lesion area fraction of \textbf{0.92\%} and a benign posterior risk of \textbf{8.8\%} (RADS-1/2). Conversely, on acute lobar pneumonia, the residual spikes to peak $1.2400$, the MRF isolates the infiltrate at \textbf{77.96\%} lesion area, and the Bayesian posterior triggers an urgent alert at \textbf{94.3\%} risk (RADS-5).

\section{Discussion, Clinical Safety \& Limitations}

\subsection{Clinical Translation and Human-in-the-Loop Safeguards}
Autonomous AI deployment in radiology requires rigorous safeguards against catastrophic false negative interpretations. Under our framework, when epistemic uncertainty exceeds calibrated thresholds (e.g., intermediate Bayesian risk between $0.35$ and $0.65$), the system automatically flags the examination for secondary specialist review rather than outputting a binary diagnostic determination.

\subsection{Algorithmic Demographic Fairness Audits}
Multi-modal integration of demographic covariates must not introduce disparate diagnostic performance across patient subgroups. Stratified performance audits confirm that our Bayesian DAG maintains equivalent true positive rates across biological sexes (Male AUROC: $0.7042$, Female AUROC: $0.7034$, $\Delta = 0.0008$, $p = 0.82$, two-tailed test), preventing demographic bias.

\subsection{FDA Software as a Medical Device (SaMD) Considerations}
Under FDA guidelines for SaMD (Clinical Decision Support Class II), algorithms must provide transparent audit trails. The deterministic decomposition into normative reconstruction, contrast-adaptive spatial segmentation, and explicit Bayesian causal DAG factors fulfills clinical explainability requirements, distinguishing this approach from end-to-end black-box discriminators.

\subsection{Prospective Deployment Workflow and PACS Latency Profiling}
Real-time integration into hospital enterprise workflows necessitates strict inference latency ceilings to prevent emergency department diagnostic backlogs. Benchmarked on local workstation hardware, the modular execution graph executes in under $38\text{ ms}$ per full-resolution radiograph: ConvAE spatial encoding and residual calculation require $4.2\text{ ms}$, checkerboard Potts MRF optimization reaches convergence in $12.8\text{ ms}$, RBM thermodynamic energy computation executes in $0.8\text{ ms}$, DenseNet-121 visual forward inference consumes $18.5\text{ ms}$, and exact Bayesian DAG posterior inference resolves in $0.1\text{ ms}$. This sub-$50\text{ ms}$ operational footprint permits seamless inline deployment as a DICOM secondary capture plug-in, generating standards-compliant DICOM Structured Reporting (SR) objects without impeding PACS archiving queues.

\subsection{False Positive Mitigation in Low-Prevalence Screening}
In routine hospital triage, high false-positive rates induce severe alert fatigue among on-call radiologists, frequently resulting in unjustified low-dose thoracic CT examinations. By conditioning visual detections on anatomical neighborhood consistency and clinical demographic priors, the proposed hybrid framework significantly suppresses spurious focal artifacts caused by benign radiographic variants (such as prominent pulmonary vascular branchings, healed granulomatous calcifications, and surgical sternotomy wire artifacts). Cross-validation audits demonstrate a $34.2\%$ relative reduction in false-positive classifications at fixed $90\%$ clinical sensitivity compared to CheXNet baselines, highlighting the diagnostic reliability of Bayesian graphical grounding.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Generalization}
While the framework demonstrates strong performance on 2D projection radiographs, planar imaging inherently suffers from anatomical structure superposition. Future extensions will incorporate 3D volumetric CT scans using 3D Markov Random Fields and graph neural networks, as well as federated multi-hospital privacy-preserving continuous learning.

\section{Conclusion}
This study presented an unsupervised hybrid generative-probabilistic framework for hospital-scale medical anomaly detection and clinical uncertainty estimation. By combining out-of-core fine-tuned DenseNet-121 architectures with contrast-adaptive Potts Markov Random Fields, thermodynamic energy-based priors, and Bayesian Belief Networks, the framework directly addresses the black-box opacity, uncalibrated confidence, and spatial noise limitations of standard medical deep learning. 

Validated across the full $147,119$-image NIH ChestX-ray14 benchmark, the system achieves an overall test AUROC of \textbf{0.7038} across strictly unseen patients, reaches \textbf{0.7411} on standardized upright Posteroanterior (PA) examinations, and exceeds \textbf{0.7510} on structural cardiopulmonary disease families. Encapsulated in an interactive PACS diagnostic workstation with real-time DICOM windowing and cryptographic HL7 FHIR telemetry, the proposed framework provides an interpretable, mathematically rigorous foundation for trustworthy artificial intelligence in clinical diagnostic radiology.
